AC Leak Water Damage Help in Winston-Salem

All day long an AC system wrings humidity from indoor air, and the resulting water needs a way out. In a healthy system the coil sheds it into a pan, and a narrow drain line carries it away. Algae and dust can plug the line, and then the pan fills up and overflows. If the air handler sits in a hallway closet or the attic, that water ends up in the nearby floor, wall or ceiling.

Slow leaks do more damage than people expect. The water wicks into drywall, soaks the platform under the unit and creeps under flooring, often with mold already started by the time a stain shows. Tracking the full extent of the moisture, drying it and making repairs is our part. An HVAC technician still has to fix the unit or unclog the line, and we plan our work around that appointment.

Do This First

What to Do Right Now

  1. Turn Off the ACNo more condensate forms once the AC is turned off at the thermostat. Flip the air handler's breaker too when water is close to its wiring.
  2. Keep Clear of Wet ElectricalCeiling lights, outlets and the unit itself are off limits while wet, and stay that way until their circuit has been turned off.
  3. Clear Water From the PanIf you can reach the pan safely, soak up or wet-vac the standing water so it stops spilling over. Crawling over a wet attic to get there is not worth it.
  4. Soak Up What You CanBlot wet carpet, wipe hard floors, set a bucket under any ceiling drip, and move boxes, clothes and furniture away from the closet or the stained area.
  5. Bring In Two ProsYou need two calls: one to us so we can measure the wet area, one to an HVAC technician who will unclog the drain. Until that repair is done, the unit stays off.

Coverage

Does Insurance Cover It?

Coverage for an AC leak usually comes down to timing. Water damage is paid for under many homeowners policies when the overflow was sudden and you responded as soon as you discovered it. If the adjuster decides the drip went on for weeks or months, it may be treated as a gradual leak or a maintenance issue and denied. Either way, the cost of fixing the AC unit is typically left out. Check your policy, and we can supply readings and photos for the claim.

Cost

What Drives the Price

  • Length of the leak, counted from the first drip to the day the system was stopped.
  • How badly the platform holding the unit is damaged.
  • How dirty the condensate became while it sat, which sets the level of cleaning.
  • Mold growth, if any, and the containment and removal it calls for.

The Usual Culprits

Why This Happens

  • The drain pipe for condensate blocked by a buildup of dust and algae.
  • Poorly connected drain piping, which lets water out before it reaches the outlet.
  • A dirty filter or low refrigerant, both of which make the coil ice up.
  • No working float switch to stop the system when the pan filled.
  • Units that rely on a condensate pump, when that pump quits or gets unplugged.

What Happens if You Wait

Attic Insulation

Overflow in the attic sinks into the surrounding insulation, which keeps the drywall under it wet. Soaked sections usually come out, since they no longer insulate.

The Platform and Closet Walls

In a closet, the unit usually rests on a wooden stand built above the return air box. Water rots that platform and wicks up the surrounding drywall, where it stays hidden behind the unit.

Baseboards and Door Trim

Trim at floor level, including the closet door frame, wicks up the leak. Because pressed-wood trim puffs up and falls apart when wet, new material goes in.

Drywall Below the Air Handler

When the air handler is overhead, the ceiling under it takes the water. The ceiling stains first, then sags, and a section soaked for days can fall into the hallway or bedroom below.
