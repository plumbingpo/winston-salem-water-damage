Dealing With Roof Leak Water Damage in Winston-Salem

The spot on your ceiling is seldom directly below the hole in the roof. A loose shingle, a failed flashing joint or an aging seal around a skylight lets rain in, and it travels across the roof deck and framing until it falls into the insulation. A ceiling stain tends to arrive late, well after the space overhead first got wet. Locating all of the moisture and drying it is our job.

To be clear, roof repair is not what we do, water damage is. Our job is the inside of the house: pulling out soaked insulation, drying the framing and drywall, treating for mold and putting the ceiling back together. When the roof is still letting water through, a temporary tarp is something we can install, and we will coordinate with your roofer so the repair comes before the ceiling is closed.

Before We Arrive

  1. Cut Power to Wet FixturesIf water is dripping through a light fixture, a ceiling fan or near an outlet, switch off that circuit at the breaker and leave the fixture alone.
  2. Catch the DripsSet buckets or pans under active drips and move furniture, rugs and electronics out of the way. Plastic or towels spread on the floor will keep it from soaking.
  3. Drain a Bulging CeilingIf the ceiling is holding a pocket of water, stand to one side and poke a small hole at the lowest point so it drains into a bucket.
  4. Leave the Roof AloneDo not climb onto a wet roof or crawl through a dark attic. Note from inside where the water is coming in and leave the rest to professionals.
  5. Photograph the DamageBefore anything is moved or discarded, get video and photos of the attic, the ceiling and damaged items, and then phone us so drying can begin.

Insurance

Does Insurance Cover It?

When a leak happens suddenly and by accident, for example a branch going through the roof or wind stripping shingles, many homeowners policies pay for the water damage inside. Leaks blamed on age, wear or skipped maintenance are usually denied, and the roof repair itself may be handled differently from the interior. We document the damage with photos and moisture readings for your adjuster, but coverage depends on your policy, so read it or ask your agent.

Why It Happens

How It Usually Starts

  • A skylight whose curb or seal has dried and cracked, so rain gets past it.
  • A valley liner that has worn through at the point where the most runoff passes.
  • A fallen limb or large hail that punctures or bruises the roof covering.
  • Flashing that has rusted, lifted or pulled away around chimneys, vents and walls.
  • Wind-torn, curling or split shingles that leave the underlayment open to rain.
What Is at Risk

Where the Damage Spreads

Inside the Walls

Water that reaches the top of a wall runs down inside it, wetting insulation and the back of the drywall. You might notice nothing more than a peeling patch or a puffy baseboard.

Drywall Ceilings

Drywall that stays wet stains, sags and loses its strength. Seams split, the tape lets go, and a heavy enough section can drop with little warning.

Hidden Attic Mold

Damp framing, paper-faced drywall, warmth and darkness give mold what it needs. It may take hold in 24 to 48 hours and work its way over the underside of the sheathing.

Lights and Wiring

Leak water heads for openings in the ceiling, such as recessed lights, fan boxes and junction boxes. Moisture in a fixture can cause a short and later corrosion, which is why it needs inspection before use.
