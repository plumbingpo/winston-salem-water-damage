Bathroom Water Damage: What Winston-Salem Homeowners Should Know

A bathroom is designed to handle water on its surfaces, never below them. Once caulk splits, grout thins out or the toilet seal gives up, each shower and flush sends a bit of water through. It collects in the subfloor and the wall behind the tile, out of sight. Our Winston-Salem team finds where the water is getting in, measures how far it has gone, and dries or removes what is wet.

These leaks are usually slow, so the damage is often deeper and older than it appears. The tile can look perfectly fine while the subfloor beneath the toilet has gone soft clear through. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map the wet area without tearing out more than we need to, then open only the sections that cannot be dried in place.

Signs to Look For

  • A hollow sound, movement, or loose tiles in the floor by the bathtub
  • A warped or swollen cabinet floor under the vanity sink
  • The caulk line at the shower or tub has split, pulled away, or turned black
  • Edges of the vinyl floor peeling up, or dark discoloration showing through it
  • The room keeps a damp, musty smell even right after a thorough cleaning
  • A brown ring or sagging spot on the ceiling directly below the bathroom
Why Speed Matters

The Damage Timeline

The first few days

Water Slips Through

With every shower, the bad caulk or grout lets a small amount of water through. Nothing shows yet, but the subfloor edge and the board behind the tile are absorbing it.

First few weeks

The Subfloor Never Dries

Between showers, the wood below the tile has no time to dry out. Within the wall cavity, and across the bottom of the floor, mold begins.

One to three months

Signs Start to Appear

Grout darkens, tiles loosen, and the caulk line turns black. A stain shows on the ceiling of any room below and slowly grows.

Six months and beyond

Structure Weakens

Around the tub or toilet the subfloor goes soft, and the joists may start rotting. At this point the job means new tile and carpentry on top of the drying.

How We Fix It

Our Bathroom Water Damage Process

1

Find the Leak

We test the tub, shower, toilet and supply lines one at a time, and use moisture meters and thermal imaging to see where water has traveled under tile and inside walls.

2

Open What Is Needed

We take out rotted subfloor, soft wall board and tile that has come loose. A toilet leak means contamination, so anything porous that will not come clean is removed.

3

Dry the Cavities

The subfloor, the framing in the walls and the space above the lower ceiling are dried with air movers and dehumidifiers. Nothing is sealed back up until we have treated with antimicrobial and the readings show dry.

4

Rebuild and Seal

We replace subfloor and backer board, and can coordinate tile, a new toilet seal, fresh caulk and ceiling repair so the leak path is closed, not covered over.

Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Leaking Shower Pan

Every use of the shower sends water beneath its floor when the pan or liner is cracked. Up top everything looks fine, yet the framing underneath never dries.

Rotted Floor at the Toilet

Each flush leaks a small amount once the wax ring fails. It is category 3 water, and the wood surrounding the flange decays until the toilet wobbles.

Damage Downstairs

Water follows pipes and joists before it drips, so the stain downstairs may sit several feet from the actual leak. Its path can cross electrical wiring and ceiling lights.

Mold Behind Tile

Neither grout nor tile is a waterproof barrier without help. Mold takes hold on the wall board once that hidden layer remains wet, and no scrubbing can get to it.
