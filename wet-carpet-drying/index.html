Wet Carpet Drying: What Winston-Salem Homeowners Should Know

There is always more water in a soaked carpet than the surface suggests. The face fibers can feel only damp while the pad underneath is soaked like a sponge and the subfloor below it is wet too. Towels and a shop vacuum reach the top layer and little else. For Winston-Salem homes, our crew handles the extraction, meters the carpet, pad and subfloor, and runs drying gear that gets to the bottom layer.

Whether the carpet can be saved depends on two things, how clean the water was and how long it has been wet. Carpet soaked by a clean supply line can usually be dried in place if we get to it quickly. With an appliance leak, which is gray water, we often discard the pad, then clean and treat the carpet. Both layers, carpet and pad, are removed when the water was sewage or came in from outside flooding.

Signs to Look For

  • Socks that come away damp, or a squishing sound, when you cross the carpet
  • Under the wet section, the floor gives a little or squeaks when you step on it
  • Lift a corner and the backing peels away from the carpet
  • Loose, wavy sections where the tack strip no longer holds the carpet
  • Brown or yellow stains wicking up to the tips of the fibers as it dries
  • Wet tack strip showing through as dark, rusty streaks next to the baseboard
Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

First hours

Water Moves Outward

Under the surface, the pad pulls water outward, well beyond what looks wet. It moves under walls and into the next room, and the tack strip starts soaking it up.

24 to 48 hours

Smell and Mold Start

That sour wet carpet odor shows up as bacteria multiply in the damp pad. Mold can start to grow on dust caught deep in the pile and on the carpet back.

Day 3 to 7

The Carpet Breaks Down

Ripples appear and the carpet delaminates because the latex in its backing is breaking down. The subfloor swells and the tack strip nails begin to rust.

Past one week

Too Late to Save

By now the carpet and pad usually have to come out. Often mold has spread to the subfloor and the base of the drywall, which adds to the job.

Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Swollen Subfloor

Subfloors made of OSB or particleboard puff up and fall apart if they remain wet. Weeks later, with the spill long forgotten, you get raised seams, squeaks and soft spots.

Water Held in the Pad

Pad can hold many times its weight in water. Long after the top feels dry, that pad is still passing moisture up into the carpet and down into the floor.

Water Quality Drops

A clean water spill does not remain clean for long. It collects bacteria and dirt from the pad and fibers, which can turn a simple drying job into a removal.

Damp Base of the Walls

Water that runs under the carpet reaches the base of the walls. It wicks up into the baseboards and drywall unnoticed, since the carpet hides that edge.

Our Process

Our Wet Carpet Drying Process

1

Find the Edges

Our first job is finding the outer limit of the water, under walls and in closets too, using a thermal camera and moisture meters. Then we determine which category of water it is, as that tells us what is safe to keep.

2

Extract the Water

With weighted extractors, carpet and the pad beneath it are pressed and emptied in one pass. Drying goes faster afterward, because far more water comes out this way than with only a wand.

3

Dry All the Layers

Depending on the job, we float the carpet on moving air or lift it and remove the pad. Our air movers and dehumidifiers keep going until meters show a dry carpet and a dry subfloor.

4

Clean and Reset

Last, we apply antimicrobial to carpet and subfloor if they need it, replace any pad that was taken out, stretch the carpet back over the tack strip, and give it a cleaning.
