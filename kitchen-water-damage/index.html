Kitchen Water Damage: What Winston-Salem Homeowners Should Know

A kitchen has more water connections than any other room: the faucet, the drain, the disposal, the dishwasher and the refrigerator line. Most of these connections are tucked into cabinets or hidden at the back of appliances. When one drips, the water runs to the back of the cabinet base and spreads under the floor. Our Winston-Salem crew locates the leak, dries what it wet, and keeps as much of your kitchen intact as we can.

In a wet kitchen, the cabinets are where the real expense lies. Plywood and solid wood will often dry successfully, whereas a particleboard box puffs up quickly and never returns to size. By removing toe kicks and drilling small holes where they will not show, we push dry air beneath and behind the cabinets, and in many kitchens the countertops and cabinets never move.

Warning Signs

  • Swelling and flaking where the cabinet sides meet the floor
  • Along the cabinet line or beside the fridge, vinyl that feels soft or looks stained
  • Stale, damp air that hits you when the doors under the sink are opened
  • Doors on the cabinets that have gone out of alignment or started sticking
  • Water stains or bubbled paint on the wall behind the sink or dishwasher
  • Laminate or wood floor that is lifting or cupping where you stand at the dishwasher
What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Behind the Dishwasher

Slowly dripping supply or drain fittings soak the cabinet sides beside the unit and the subfloor below. None of it is visible behind the appliance until the flooring starts to change.

Layers of Flooring

Many kitchens have a newer floor installed on top of an older one. Between those layers water is trapped, unable to evaporate and invisible from above.

Refrigerator Water Line

The thin tube feeding the ice maker can leak a few drops a minute. When the fridge never gets moved, this amount will rot the flooring and the wall.

The Wall Behind the Cabinets

Supply and drain lines run through the wall behind the sink and dishwasher. A leak there wets drywall and framing that the cabinets cover completely, so mold can spread across the wall unseen.

The Clock

The Damage Timeline

First hour

Water Runs Under

When a hose or fitting fails, water heads behind the cabinets and beneath the appliances. Through edges and seams, it works its way under the floor covering.

24 to 48 hours

Cabinets Start Swelling

Particleboard cabinet bases and toe kicks soak up water and swell. In the dark, enclosed gap below the cabinets, and at the back of the dishwasher, mold can start.

Days 3 through 7

Floors Begin Moving

As adhesive releases, vinyl begins lifting, while hardwood boards cup and laminate edges swell. Cabinet doors and drawers drift out of line.

After a week

Removal Becomes Likely

A cabinet box that has swollen is not coming back. Subfloor may be delaminating, and mold behind the cabinets often means removing them along with the countertop above.

Our Process

Our Kitchen Water Damage Process

1

Shut Off the Leak

We shut off the leaking fixture or appliance line, pull the dishwasher or refrigerator if needed, and confirm exactly which connection failed so it can be repaired before drying begins.

2

Map the Moisture

Using moisture meters and thermal imaging, we check cabinet bases, the wall behind them, and the flooring across the room. Under kitchen flooring, water often goes farther than anyone expects.

3

Dry Without Demolition

We remove the toe kicks and direct air behind and beneath the cabinets. An antimicrobial is applied to damp cavities while air movers and dehumidifiers work over several days.

4

Take Out Only What Failed

We carefully remove any flooring, subfloor or cabinet section that cannot be saved. After dry readings are confirmed, the appliances go back in and we help you arrange the remaining repairs.
