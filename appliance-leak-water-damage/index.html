Dealing With Appliance Leak Water Damage in Winston-Salem

Three appliances cause most of the leaks we see. Washer supply hoses can burst and keep spraying at full pressure until the valve is closed. Dishwashers usually leak a little at a time, at the drain hose or door seal, hidden beneath the cabinets. A refrigerator ice maker line is a thin tube that can drip behind the unit for months. No two of these call for the same cleanup.

On every Winston-Salem job, the first step is moving the appliance and tracing the path the water took. In kitchens that usually means under the cabinets and beneath the flooring. In laundry rooms it means the wall behind the machine and the rooms next door, or the ceiling below if the laundry is upstairs. Standing water is extracted, absorbed moisture is measured, and drying equipment is sized to fit the wet area.

First Five Minutes

Before We Arrive

  1. Turn Off the ApplianceCancel the cycle, and pull the plug only when the floor and the outlet near it are dry. When either one is wet, turn the power off at the breaker.
  2. Shut Off the Water LineWashers have hot and cold valves on the wall behind them. Dishwashers shut off under the sink. A refrigerator line has a small valve below or behind the unit.
  3. Soak Up Standing WaterUse towels or a mop on whatever water is within reach, which keeps it from spreading beneath flooring and cabinets. Do not vacuum unless the machine is rated for wet and dry use.
  4. Keep the Door ClosedIf the washer or dishwasher has not drained, leave it shut. When the door opens mid-cycle, the full load of water can pour out on the floor.
  5. Photograph the Failed PartPhotograph the failed fitting, valve or hose, and hold on to the part itself. Both the appliance maker and your insurer may want to look at it.
Insurance

What About Insurance?

Homeowners policies often cover sudden and accidental events, and a burst hose or a machine overflowing with no warning is that kind of event. Payment typically goes toward the damage the water did, and the appliance or the part that failed is not included. Gradual leaks usually fall outside coverage, so slow seepage at a dishwasher or an ice maker line makes for a tougher claim. Some insurers also ask about the age and upkeep of the hose. Hold on to the part that failed, photograph everything ahead of cleanup, and review what your policy says.

Common Causes

How It Usually Starts

  • A clogged defrost drain or cracked drain pan that lets a refrigerator leak onto the floor.
  • A kinked or cracked ice maker line, or one whose fitting has worked loose.
  • Washer supply hoses made of rubber that get weak over the years and burst from the pressure.
  • Worn dishwasher door gaskets, cracked drain hoses or a failed pump seal.
  • The drain hose of the washer pulling free of the standpipe, or the standpipe backing up from a clog.
Why Speed Matters

What the Water Damages

Flooring and Subfloor

Water slides beneath laminate, vinyl plank and hardwood. Cupping and swelling show up in the boards, and the subfloor can stay damp long after the surface appears dry.

Wall Bases and Trim

Water standing on a laundry or kitchen floor wicks up into baseboards and the bottom of the drywall. Often the worst of it is on the wall in back of the appliance, where it went unseen.

Rooms Next Door

A washer hose can release water for hours. That water moves beneath walls into bedrooms, closets and hallways, soaking pad and carpet far from the laundry room.

Ceiling Below an Upstairs Laundry

The ceiling under an upper floor laundry catches whatever water makes it past the floor above. Sagging, staining and soaked insulation above the drywall are the usual result.

How We Fix It

How We Handle Appliance Leak Water Damage

1

Pull and Inspect

The appliance is disconnected and moved, and the supply is confirmed off. Then thermal imaging and moisture meters show us where water went below flooring, under the cabinets and into the walls nearby.

2

Remove Standing Water

Standing water is extracted from floors and carpet. To reach water caught below cabinets or under floating floors, our crew pulls toe kicks or takes up boards.

3

Dry in Place

We set air movers and dehumidifiers and, where possible, dry cabinets and hardwood in place with directed airflow so they do not have to be torn out.

4

Clean and Confirm

Surfaces touched by washer or dishwasher discharge get antimicrobial treatment. Final moisture readings are taken before we return the appliance, so it never sits on a damp floor.
