Hardwood Floor Water Damage: What Winston-Salem Homeowners Should Know

It does not take long for water to change a wood floor. Boards take on moisture from below and from the edges, swell against each other and start to cup, with the edges rising higher than the center. A floor in this condition is not necessarily ruined. Boards of solid wood often return to flat if they are dried during those early days. Success depends on a slow, even drying rate that draws water from the flooring and from the subfloor beneath it.

Our tool for hardwood is the floor drying mat. Each mat is sealed against the floor and hooked to a vacuum unit, which pulls moisture upward through seams and boards as dehumidifiers dry the air in the room. This takes longer than drying carpet or drywall, often a week or more. Hold off on the sander until every board is fully dry. Sand a cupped floor too soon and it will crown later.

Warning Signs

  • Sections of flooring that have risen, forming a tent shape along a seam
  • Boards shrinking apart and leaving gaps following a rushed dry out
  • Flooring that gives under your weight in the area that got wet
  • Mildew smell coming up through the boards
  • Dark gray or black stains along seams and around nail heads
  • A hazy, whitened or flaking finish where the water sat

The Clock

What Happens if You Wait

First hours

Seepage Below the Boards

Water slips through the seams and spreads between the boards and subfloor. You can towel off the top and still have a saturated underside.

Hours 24 to 48

Cupping Starts

Boards swell and the edges rise. Finish may turn cloudy. Growth is able to begin below, on raw wood and on the subfloor panels.

Day 3 to 7

Pressure Builds Up

Swelling builds pressure across the floor. Boards crush against each other, fasteners loosen and sections can begin to lift off the subfloor.

After a week

Permanent Damage

Expect a good chance of mold below, dark staining and boards that have buckled. At this stage most of the wet area needs to be replaced rather than dried.

What We Do

Our Hardwood Floor Water Damage Process

1

Take Baseline Readings

Our team meters the wet boards, the subfloor under them and a dry area of floor to compare against. That tells us how deep the water went and whether saving the floor is realistic.

2

Extract Standing Water

Standing water is extracted right away and rugs and furniture are moved off the wet area. The faster water stops feeding the seams, the less the boards swell.

3

Install the Mat System

Floor mats are taped down and connected to a vacuum system that pulls moisture up through the boards. To avoid cracking, dehumidifiers keep conditions steady and the wood dries at an even rate.

4

Monitor and Advise

We track readings every day or two until boards and subfloor match dry reference areas. At that point you hear from us if it needs nothing, some replacement boards, or a refinish.

Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Growth Beneath the Floor

No air moves and no light reaches the gap between the hardwood and the subfloor. Mold in that layer cannot be seen from the room, and a musty odor is usually the first sign.

Stains From Nails and Staples

Staples and flooring nails corrode when moisture lingers, and their grip weakens. Dark marks from that rust soak into the boards and can be too deep to sand out.

Sanding Too Early

Sanding cupped boards flat while they are still wet removes the raised edges. After the moisture leaves, each board is left with a raised center, which is crowning.

Moisture Under the Boards

Long after the boards seem dry, the subfloor may still be holding water. If it is not dried too, the floor will cup again or grow mold from underneath.
