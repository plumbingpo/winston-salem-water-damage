No part of restoring a water-damaged building matters more than structural drying, yet it is also where untrained crews make the most mistakes. After extraction removes the standing water, significant moisture remains trapped in structural materials, including drywall, wood framing, subflooring, concrete, insulation and other building components. Controlled evaporation and dehumidification, applied scientifically, are needed to get that moisture out before a wet structure develops mold, rotting wood, degraded materials and odors.

Winston-Salem Restoration Co employs IICRC-certified structural drying technicians who understand psychrometry, the study of temperature, humidity and moisture relationships in air. For each job they apply it to calculate airflow patterns, temperature settings and the placement of equipment that will give the best conditions for evaporation. Plaster, concrete, hardwood and drywall each dry at their own rate, so each needs its own approach to get down to an acceptable moisture level with no added damage.

Our structural drying process includes daily moisture monitoring with calibrated moisture meters, thermo-hygrometers and thermal imaging cameras. Readings are recorded from start to finish in a detailed drying log, which shows moisture dropping step by step in every affected material. Each day we use those numbers to reposition or retune the equipment so drying stays efficient. We call a job dry when every affected material meets the moisture standard in the IICRC S500 guidelines, and never just because a surface feels dry.

Why Structural Drying Is Not a DIY Job

There is real science behind structural drying, which is why guessing does not work. The leading cause of secondary mold after a water event is setting out a few fans and trusting they will be enough. It involves daily monitoring, a calculation of the required grain depression (the capacity to remove moisture) and a working knowledge of the way materials absorb moisture and let it go. Lacking that understanding, people either stop too soon and leave moisture for mold, or dry too hard and harm wood and other materials.

Professional drying equipment is not a bigger version of what stores sell, it is a different kind of machine. High-velocity air movers produce precise airflow patterns that speed up evaporation from the structure, and low-grain refrigerant (LGR) commercial dehumidifiers capture 10 to 20 times the moisture a home unit can. Machines like these cost a great deal and take training to use well, which explains why professional results are so much better than a do-it-yourself effort.

Signs You Need Structural Drying in Winston-Salem

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Humidity that will not come down in the affected space, whatever ventilation you try
  • Gaps opening up in a hardwood floor, or planks with raised edges or centers
  • Musty odors that continue after extraction, which suggests trapped moisture
  • Baseboards or door trim that swell, warp or pull away from the wall
  • Moisture meter readings above normal in walls, floors or ceilings after water damage
  • Beads of moisture on windows and cold surfaces after the water event
  • Wallboard with water stains, puffiness or a spongy feel

Maintenance Tips for Structural Drying

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Air movers need a clear path, so avoid stacking things on the equipment or in front of it
  • Address any water intrusion immediately, because the faster drying begins, the better the outcome
  • Make sure the technician can get in each day for monitoring visits
  • Every hour of run time matters, so resist switching the machines off for some quiet
  • If a new damp spot or smell shows up while drying is underway, let the technician know at once
  • Until a technician removes the equipment, keep it running day and night without breaks
