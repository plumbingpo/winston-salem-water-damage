Wall and Drywall Water Damage: What Winston-Salem Homeowners Should Know

Think of drywall as a wick. When water reaches the bottom of a wall, the gypsum and paper draw it upward, often a foot or more above the level the water ever reached on the floor. From the room, the surface may seem dry and sound even though the hidden face and the insulation are saturated. For that reason our team relies on moisture meter readings rather than appearance.

Not every wet wall has to be torn out. When the water was clean and the drywall remains firm, drying it where it stands is often possible, at times by pulling the baseboard and pushing air into the cavity through small holes. Walls that are mushy, falling apart, moldy or contaminated get a flood cut instead, where our crew cuts straight across above the damage and takes out whatever sits under that line.

What It Looks Like

  • Fuzzy or dark patches hidden in corners or in back of furniture
  • Swollen or warped baseboards with a gap opening behind them
  • Mildew smell you notice most when standing close to one section of wall
  • Cool, slightly clammy drywall next to walls at normal temperature
  • Wet tack strip, lifting planks or stained flooring at the base of the wall
  • Bubbling, wrinkled or peeling paint and wallpaper low on the wall
What You Cannot See

What Hides Behind It

Insulation Holding Water

Once wet, fiberglass insulation stays wet and keeps the studs damp. A wall with wet insulation will not dry from the room side alone.

Walls You Cannot See

Sections of wall covered by cabinets, a vanity or shelving stay damp because air never reaches them. Checking under the cabinet base and on the reverse face tells us if it is wet.

Mold Inside Cavities

The back of the drywall is dark, still and lined with paper, which is ideal for mold. You can have a clean looking painted surface with heavy growth right behind it.

Wet Outlets and Wiring

Outlets and wiring are mounted near the floor, exactly where the water gathers. Leave the outlets on a damp wall alone until someone has checked the circuit.

Our Process

Our Wall and Drywall Water Damage Process

1

Map the Moisture

We scan each wall with a moisture meter and thermal camera and mark how high and how far the water traveled, including the opposite side and adjoining rooms.

2

Decide What Stays

Our call on each section, dry it or take it out, depends on water category, exposure time and the condition of the panel, and we explain it in plain terms.

3

Open Up or Air Out

Walls we are keeping have their baseboards removed so air can be pushed into the cavity. For walls being removed, we make a level flood cut and take out wet insulation.

4

Dry and Rebuild

Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on, usually 3 to 5 days, until the studs and the drywall left in place test dry. After that comes insulation, new drywall hung and finished, trim put back and paint.

If Nothing Is Done

What Happens if You Wait

First hours

Water Starts Climbing

Water at the base of the wall soaks into the drywall edge and starts to climb. Both the baseboard and the bottom plate start absorbing water.

Hours 24 to 48

Wall Swells

You may see blistered paint and swollen trim as the damp area moves up the wall. Growth is able to get going on the paper that lines the hidden side.

Days 3 through 7

Panels Lose Strength

Gypsum softens and the paper lets go. Insulation slumps inside the wall, odor builds and mold spreads across the hidden back side.

Past one week

Framing Is Affected

Studs and plates stay wet long enough to grow mold and begin to decay. A far larger section of wall needs tearing out and rebuilding.
