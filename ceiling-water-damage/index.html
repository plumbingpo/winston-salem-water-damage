Ceiling Water Damage Repair Help in Winston-Salem

A brown ring on the ceiling is the last thing to show up, not the first. Before any mark shows, water has traveled down a joist, saturated the insulation and collected on top of the drywall. The leak may be a bathroom on the floor above, a pipe in the ceiling, an air conditioner drain line or the roof. Our team finds where it starts before any patching is done.

Covering the mark with paint is not a repair. While the space overhead stays damp, the stain returns, and within 24 to 48 hours mold can begin on the drywall paper. Our crew removes no more ceiling than necessary, takes out soaked insulation, dries the framing and the drywall that stays, and finishes with a patch, texture and paint that match. A sagging ceiling is a different matter. Do not stand beneath it, and get help on the way.

What It Looks Like

  • Discolored rings overhead that get bigger each time it rains or the bathroom is used
  • Water trails on the upper wall, starting at the ceiling corner
  • Ceiling paint lifting off in blisters or peeling strips
  • Joint tape coming loose, with screws and panel edges telegraphing through
  • New fine cracks that radiate from a discolored patch
  • Stale, mildew like odor you cannot trace to anything you can see

How We Fix It

How We Handle Ceiling Water Damage Repair

1

Find the Source

Using thermal imaging and moisture meters, our team follows the water to its origin and inspects the attic or room overhead. The leak has to be stopped before repairs make sense.

2

Make It Safe

Power to wet fixtures is shut off and furniture below is covered or moved. Where water is trapped overhead, it gets drained under control into containers.

3

Open and Dry

Damaged drywall and wet insulation come out. Drying equipment, meaning a dehumidifier and air movers, works on the framing and adjacent drywall, with antimicrobial treatment for water that was not clean.

4

Repair and Finish

After the meter shows normal levels, new insulation and drywall go up, joints are taped and finished, the texture is matched and a stain blocking primer goes on ahead of paint.

Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Mold Above You

Even when the ceiling feels dry to the touch, the batts and the unseen face of the drywall remain damp, giving mold a hidden place to spread.

Older Ceiling Materials

Some older popcorn textures and plasters contain asbestos. Testing comes first in such a home, ahead of any cutting, scraping or demolition of the damaged section.

Electricity Overhead

Recessed lights, fans and junction boxes sit right in the wet zone. A short is possible when water enters a light, which is why the breaker should be shut off.

Sudden Collapse

Water adds a lot of weight to a panel while weakening its hold on the framing. An entire piece can fall from a bulging spot onto anything below.

Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

The first few hours

Water Spreads Above

Moisture follows the joists and the insulation drinks it in. Because the panel soaks from its top side, nothing may look wrong from underneath.

Hours 24 to 48

Stains and Bubbles

Discoloration shows up and the paint begins bubbling. Mold can begin on the drywall paper and the wet insulation presses down on the panel.

Days 3 through 7

The Ceiling Bows

Drywall softens and pulls away from its screws. A stale odor drifts into the room as seams separate and the drywall sags in the spans between framing.

Past one week

Sections May Fall

Waterlogged sections can fall, bringing wet insulation with them. Mold is established in the cavity and the repair now covers a much larger area.
