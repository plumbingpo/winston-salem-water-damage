Attic Water Damage in Winston-Salem

There are two ways an attic ends up wet. From above, rain enters through failed flashing, worn shingles, or the seals at chimneys and vents. The house itself sends moisture upward too, and in a poorly ventilated attic it condenses on cold sheathing. Whichever it is, few people ever go up and look. Our team in Winston-Salem inspects the attic, finds the source, and dries it out.

Most attic jobs center on wet insulation. Once wet, both cellulose and fiberglass lose R-value, and heating and cooling the home costs more. Even worse, it keeps water pressed against the framing and the ceiling drywall for weeks. We remove what is saturated, dry the wood with air movers and dehumidifiers, and treat any mold on the rafters or sheathing.

Signs to Look For

  • Rusty nail tips poking through the sheathing, sometimes with water beads on them
  • Flattened, matted or discolored insulation that stands out from what surrounds it
  • Brown rings or stains on an upstairs ceiling, often worse after rain
  • Rafters and roof decking marked with dark streaks or stains
  • Spots of daylight at a chimney, a vent pipe, or a valley in the roof
  • Attic ducts and metal hangers showing drips, rust or water marks
Hidden Risks

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Bath Fans Venting Inside

Every day, a fan duct that stops short of the roof releases humid bathroom air into the attic. On cold sheathing that air condenses, and the result is easy to mistake for a leaking roof.

Ceiling Collapse

Soaked insulation carries a great deal of weight. The ceiling drywall under it weakens where it is fastened, and it can give way suddenly and bring the wet insulation along.

Insulation That Stops Working

Insulation that is damp and compressed barely insulates, and drying may not bring it back. Rooms under the wet section run hotter or colder, and energy bills rise.

Mold Under the Roof Deck

With poor ventilation, mold commonly grows on the bottom of the roof deck. A wide area may be covered by the time somebody climbs up to check.

How We Handle Attic Water Damage

We check the roof deck, flashing points, vents and exhaust ducts to learn whether the water is a leak, condensation, or both. Its full reach is mapped with a thermal camera and meter readings.

We bag and remove insulation that is moldy or saturated, which lets the drywall and wood beneath it dry. Rooms under the attic are protected, and the route in and out stays contained.

The Clock

What Happens if You Wait

The first storm

Insulation Takes It

Rain that gets past the roof lands in the insulation, which absorbs it. Nothing shows indoors, so the leak goes unnoticed and the roof is not repaired.

24 to 48 hours

Mold Can Start

Damp wood and paper facing in a warm, still attic are ideal for mold. Growth can begin on the sheathing and on the back of the ceiling drywall.

A few weeks on

Stains Come Through

Once storm after storm has saturated the insulation, the ceiling starts getting wet. The drywall may sag, and rings of brown staining appear on the ceiling of the room below.

After months

Wood Starts Rotting

Roofing nails loosen as the sheathing delaminates. Mold spreads across large sections of the roof deck, and the rafters remain damp enough to decay.
