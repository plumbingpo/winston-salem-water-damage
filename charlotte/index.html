How Drying Works on a Charlotte Water Damage Job

A common question from Charlotte customers is what to expect once the machines are running. On day one, the crew extracts the water, removes anything that cannot be dried, and sets air movers and dehumidifiers in the affected rooms. The equipment is loud, about like several large fans, and it makes the room warm. It needs to stay on around the clock, and turning it off, shifting it or airing out the room slows everything down.

Each day after that, a technician visits to take moisture readings in the same spots and record them. Equipment is repositioned toward the slower spots as others finish, and surplus units leave the house. You will see the numbers and hear what they mean.

Drying usually lasts 3 to 5 days. It can run longer when water sat for a while before it was found, when it reached insulation or hardwood, or when several floors are involved. When every reading matches the dry standard, Winston-Salem Restoration Co removes the equipment and gives you the drying record for your Charlotte property. Only then should rebuilding start, because closing up damp walls traps the problem inside.

Common Water Damage Issues in Charlotte

Water usually escapes at the same few vulnerable spots in a home. These are the ones Charlotte property owners report to us again and again.

Water heaters: Over the years, mineral deposits settle in a tank and corrode the metal underneath. The first sign is often a rusty puddle or a damp ring on the floor around the base. A split tank does not stop at one tankful, because the inlet keeps feeding it until the valve is shut. When the unit lives in an attic or interior closet, the water reaches carpets and ceilings far from it.

Roofs: One strong storm is enough to peel back shingles and push water beneath the roof covering. Leaks also start around chimneys, vent pipes and skylights where the sealant has dried out. Since water follows the framing downhill, the ceiling stain rarely sits directly below the actual opening. On Charlotte roof leak calls, our crew removes saturated insulation, sets drying equipment and can cover the opening until a roofer arrives.

Freezing: Depending on the season, a hard overnight freeze can burst lines that were fine the day before. The first to go are hose bibs, irrigation lines and any pipe routed through an unheated space. Vacant homes and rooms with the heat turned down are especially exposed. No flow or a bare trickle at the tap on a frigid day means a frozen line, so close the main and get help.

Backups: A drain that gurgles or several fixtures that slow down together point to a blockage in the main line. Once it closes off fully, anything you flush or drain returns at the lowest opening in the house. The water is unsanitary, and hard surfaces need disinfecting after the solids and liquid are removed. Stay out of the affected rooms and avoid running water until the line is cleared.

Seepage: A wet line along the bottom of a foundation wall is a sign that water is building up in the soil next to it. The usual reasons are grading that tilts inward, clogged perimeter drains and downspouts that empty too close. Early clues include powdery white residue on the concrete, flaking wall paint and air that always smells musty. The team at Winston-Salem Restoration Co can extract standing water and dry the space, and we will tell you what we think let it in.

How Charlotte Homeowners Can Avoid Water Damage

Houses tell you when water is getting where it should not. The signs are easy to miss if you do not know them, so here is what our technicians look for in Charlotte homes. Blistering paint, ceiling stains, warped baseboards and floorboards that curl at the edges each point to water behind or beneath them. Add a stale odor in an enclosed space, a loose toilet, or a faucet that keeps dripping.

None of these is an emergency on its own, but each is cheaper to fix now than later. Re-caulk tubs and showers when the bead cracks, since water behind tile does damage you will not see for months. If you have a sump pump, pour a bucket of water into the pit a few times a year to confirm it switches on. Keep stored items off the basement or garage floor on shelves or in plastic bins.

Good upkeep lowers the odds, but it cannot remove them. What it does is make them rarer and smaller, and that matters when the alternative is days of drying equipment in your Charlotte living room.
