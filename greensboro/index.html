Water Damage We See Most in Greensboro

In Greensboro, the same handful of problems accounts for nearly all the water damage we see. Knowing them makes it easier to catch trouble while it is still a small repair.

Pipes: When water freezes inside a line it can crack the metal or plastic, then pour out as soon as things warm up. Lines in exterior walls, garages and unheated attics are the usual trouble spots, depending on the season. In older homes, aging joints and tired connectors sometimes fail with no cold snap involved. Shut off the main valve right away, because an open line can soak several rooms quickly.

Appliances: The thin hoses feeding a washer, dishwasher or ice maker do not last forever. Tanks rust from the inside, so an old water heater may drip for weeks or give out suddenly. We see many of these in Greensboro homes where the unit sits upstairs or over a finished room.

Overflows: It takes only a few minutes for a blocked toilet or a forgotten faucet to flood a bathroom. From there it slips beneath the trim and can drip into whatever sits under that floor. If the overflow came from the toilet bowl or a drain, treat it as contaminated and keep children and pets away.

Roof: Rain gets in through worn flashing, damaged shingles or gutters that have stopped draining. The stain on your ceiling usually shows up well after the insulation above it is soaked. The crew from Winston-Salem Restoration Co can dry the attic and ceiling, but the roof itself needs a repair to stop the leak.

Basement: Once the ground outside is soaked, water finds its way into a basement through wall cracks and the seam where floor meets wall. If the sump pump sticks or the electricity goes out mid-storm, the pit fills and spills across the slab. Finished basements suffer most, since carpet, drywall and framing all soak up water from the bottom.

Mold: Mold can start growing within 24 to 48 hours on materials that stay wet. A lot of the mold we find in Greensboro comes from a past leak that looked dry on the surface only. A musty smell, peeling paint or dark spotting near a previous repair is worth having checked.
